# Authority Builder™ Design Library

28 complete, production-ready websites for coaches, creators, consultants and business owners.

Each one is a real small site you can deploy today: a photographic hero, named photo slots for you and your clients, working forms, a thank-you page, trust and policy pages, a launch checklist and a search audit already applied to the code. Each also comes with notes that show why it works.

## 28 Sites, 28 Designs

The library has 10 style directions, and every site inside a direction is its own design. Each one has its own palette, type pairing, section headers, card treatment, section breaks, background texture and way of moving. All 28 are set in `src/variants.py`, and every palette is checked against WCAG AA when the build runs. Each site's `notes.md` describes its variation in a sentence.

- **Movement with a job.** Sections below the first screen arrive as you scroll, in 1 of 4 quiet styles: a short rise, a short slide, a wipe or a fade. The header steps aside on the way down and returns on the way up. Pictures drift slightly as they cross the screen. Nothing moves for decoration: no count-up numbers, no tickers, no tilting cards, no shine on buttons, no glow. Anyone who asks for reduced motion gets none of it.
- **Restraint.** Only the first and last headline on a page carry the accent colour. Icons sit plainly, without tinted tiles. Buttons and cards cast no coloured glow. There are no frosted-glass cards and no colour-wash backgrounds.
- **Pictures.** 25 sites have a picture band in the middle of the page: a full-width painting, a framed painting, 3 crops beside a line of copy, or a row of crops. It repeats the main button straight after the proof.
- **A place for your own assets.** 24 sites have an asset showcase straight under the first section, in 1 of 4 forms: a video, the product on a laptop and a phone, 3 slides from a deck, or 3 social posts. Each picture in it is a named file (`video-1.jpg`, `mock-1.jpg`, `phone-1.jpg`, `slide-1.jpg`, `post-1.jpg`), so you drop in a video still, a screenshot, an exported slide or a post and it appears in the frame. The files that ship are designed stand-ins in the site's own colours, made by `python3 src/people.py --assets`. `LAUNCH.md` lists each one and its size. A video slot plays a real file when there is one: save `video-1.mp4` in the site's `media` folder, beside `images`, and rebuild. 3 sites (001, 004 and 027) ship with a 4-second sample clip, made from the site's own Pexels photograph with an AI video model. They are stand-ins for your footage, not real footage of anything.
- **The action stays in reach.** On a phone the main button sits in a bar at the bottom. On a desktop it follows the reader as a floating dock once the hero has scrolled away, and leaves when the closing section arrives.
- **Guy Incognito.** Every site belongs to the same sample owner, so you always know a demo when you see one. His photo slots show a figure in a hat and round glasses, with no face. Save your own photo over the file and he is gone.

## Open It

- **On your computer.** Download the repository and open `index.html`. Everything works offline except the web fonts and form sending.
- **On Netlify (recommended).** Create a Netlify site from this repository. Leave the build command empty: `netlify.toml` already publishes the root. Then open Forms, select Enable form detection and deploy again so the sample forms can take entries.
- **On GitHub Pages.** In the repository settings, open Pages and publish the `main` branch from the root. Every page is served. GitHub Pages has no form handling, so a sample form shows an error when it is sent, and it cannot send the header that keeps the fictional sample sites out of search.

## Launch One As Your Own Site

1. **Pick the site.** Open `index.html`, filter by who you are, and select View Live to scroll the sample on your phone.
2. **Copy its `site` folder** into a new repository or straight into Netlify. That folder is the whole website. It needs nothing else from this library.
3. **Add your photos.** Every picture is a file in `site/images/`. Save your own under the same file name and it appears everywhere that photo is used. `LAUNCH.md` lists each file, its size and the shot to take.
4. **Work through the rest of `LAUNCH.md`.** It lists exactly what to replace and connect for that site: the brand and copy, the address, the forms, any links that need your own accounts, the policies and the structured data.
5. **Run the pre-launch check** on your copy. It lists anything from the demo that is still in your files, including any placeholder photo you have not replaced.

```bash
python3 src/prelaunch.py path/to/your/site
```

In a hurry? Select Get The Code on any site. Its page has the whole main page as 1 block of code to copy, with every picture loading from this repository, so it works wherever you paste it. Under it is a build prompt you can paste into Claude with your real proof instead.

- `index.html`: the site's short page. A preview, a View Live button, the page's code to copy and a build prompt. Nothing else.

## What "Production Ready" Means Here

- **No dead ends.** Every button and link leads to a real page or section. The audit and the page checker flag any that do not.
- **Forms that send.** Every form is a real HTML form with named fields, required fields marked, a hidden spam trap and a thank-you page. They follow Netlify's pattern for plain HTML forms, so on Netlify they collect entries once form detection is on. On another host, point each form's `action` at your form or email tool.
- **Links only you can finish are listed, not hidden.** A checkout, a podcast app or a social profile needs your own account. Those links point at `example.com/connect/…` and are counted in `LAUNCH.md`, so none is forgotten.
- **Ready to be found.** The sites carry no `noindex`. The library keeps its fictional brands out of search with host settings in the root `netlify.toml`, so a site you copy out is ready for Google the moment it has your address on it.
- **A person on every page.** Your face has a place on the first screen, beside the About story, under the closing button, and on the contact, booking and thank-you pages. Client faces sit beside their words.
- **Measured, not assumed.** Before the movement and variation pass, every site scored 96 to 100 for performance and 100 for accessibility, best practices and SEO in Lighthouse's phone test, run on a local copy. Those figures, and the ones in each audit table, have not been re-measured since that pass. Run `node src/speed.mjs` and `node src/legible.mjs`, then rebuild, to refresh them. Your host will change the speed figures either way.
- **Heroes sized to the screen.** On a 1440 by 900 screen the middle hero ends 785px down, so the next section shows beneath it, and every first button is on screen. Headlines sit at about 62px on a desktop and 34px on a phone.
- **Photographs that hold up close.** Each hero is a 2560px photograph cut down to 4 sizes, so a phone, a laptop and a large screen each get a crisp file.
- **Plain words.** Every main page's body copy is measured on the Flesch-Kincaid scale. All of them read at school grade 6.2 or below, the range a large landing-page benchmark links to the highest conversion.
- **Readable over the painting.** A contrast checker cannot read text over a picture, so `src/legible.mjs` measures every line of text against the pixels behind it, on a desktop and a phone. Every line clears WCAG AA.

What a site cannot contain is you. The demo brand, the sample proof and the template policies are yours to replace, and each site says so in a visible note in its footer until you do.

## What "Researched" And "Audited" Mean Here

- **Researched.** Each page type was researched on its own: sales pages, opt-in pages, pricing, webinars, local business sites, quizzes, forms, case studies, product pages, About pages, articles, link pages, podcasts, speaker pages, events and memberships. Each finding became a change in that sample's code. `src/upgrades.py` records each finding, its source and the change. The figures are benchmarks from testing firms and software vendors, so treat them as direction and test on your own page.
- **Audited.** The Full SEO and AI Search Audit was run against every sample's files, and what failed was fixed in the code. The build prints any check that fails. Checks that need a live website are marked "Couldn't check" and were not guessed.
- **Measured.** `src/speed.mjs` runs Lighthouse on every sample and `src/legible.mjs` measures text contrast over the paintings. The results appear in each audit table.

## Rules Every Sample Follows

- **Fictional brands only.** Every name, testimonial, price and result is invented for teaching, and each site says so in its footer. Replace all of it with your own real proof. Never publish invented testimonials or numbers.
- **Real photographs of places, no real faces.** Each site's hero, and the pictures of places cut from it, is a photograph from Pexels, listed in `src/data/heroes.json` and credited in [CREDITS.md](CREDITS.md). Pexels photographs are free to use, but may not be sold or passed on as stand-alone files. No photograph shows a person's face. Every picture of a person is a placeholder painted by `src/people.py`: a back-lit figure with no face, so it can never be mistaken for a real client. The earlier paintings can still be made with `src/paint.py`.
- **Honest persuasion.** No fake countdown timers and no invented scarcity. Any limit shown is the kind that could be real: seats, dates, a calendar.
- **Search-ready code.** 1 H1, a real title and description, canonical and Open Graph tags, JSON-LD that matches the visible text, and every word in the HTML itself.
- **Real trust pages everywhere.** About, Contact, Privacy, Terms and Refunds exist and are linked from every footer, even on pages with no menu. The policy wording is a starting template: replace it and have a lawyer check it.
- **Accessible by default.** Colour pairs are checked at build time against WCAG AA. Tap targets are 48px or more. Focus states are visible. Motion respects the reduced-motion setting.
- **1 small script, for movement only.** Each page carries 1 inline script of about 3KB that runs the reveals, the counters and the action dock. Every word is in the HTML, nothing is hidden unless the script is running, and the page reads the same with scripts off. The quiz adds 1 more small script, and its questions are in the HTML either way.

## The Research

The library draws on five bodies of work, all listed with sources on the research page:

- **Behaviour and decision science:** Kahneman and Tversky, Cialdini, Fogg, Ariely, Thaler, Berger, Deci and Ryan.
- **Page and interface laws:** Nielsen Norman Group's eyetracking research and the Laws of UX.
- **Marketing and copy:** Schwartz, Ogilvy, Caples, Hopkins, Dunford, Ries and Trout, Hormozi, Brunson, Miller, Rackham, Voss.
- **Design and conversion benchmarks:** Unbounce's landing page benchmark on reading level (read second-hand and labelled that way), current writing on editorial web typography, and published reviews of high-performing coaching pages. Sources are in [CREDITS.md](CREDITS.md).
- **Search and AI answers:** Google's own documentation and current reporting on it, including the retirement of FAQ rich results on 7 May 2026 and Google's May 2026 guide to its generative AI features.

Search rules change often. Before you rely on any rule here, check Google's current documentation. If this library and Google disagree, Google wins.

## Rebuilding

The library is generated from page definitions in `src/pages`. The build itself needs only Python 3. Everything the optional tools make is committed, so you can skip them.

```bash
python3 src/build.py     # writes every site, its launch checklist, its code page and notes, the index and the research page, and runs the audit
python3 src/check.py     # checks tags, headings, structured data, links, anchors, labels and dashes on every page
python3 src/photos.py    # optional: fetches each site's hero photograph from Pexels and cuts its 4 sizes (needs Pillow)
python3 src/paint.py     # optional: the earlier painted heroes, in place of photographs (needs numpy, scipy and OpenCV)
python3 src/people.py    # optional: repaints the photo placeholders listed in src/data/photos.json (same needs, plus Pillow)
python3 src/shots.py     # optional: regenerates previews, site icons and share images (needs Google Chrome and Pillow)
node src/speed.mjs       # optional: runs Lighthouse on every site (needs Node and the lighthouse package)
node src/legible.mjs     # optional: measures text contrast over the paintings (same needs as speed.mjs)
```

After any optional tool, run `build.py` once more so the audit tables pick up the new files and measurements.

How the source is organised:

- `src/pages/`: the content of each page, section by section.
- `src/upgrades.py`: the research applied to each page type, with the source for each change, and which painting each site uses.
- `src/sections.py`, `src/kit.py`, `src/polish.py`, `src/themes.py`: the section components, shared styles and the 10 style directions.
- `src/variants.py`: what makes each of the 28 sites its own design. `src/motion.py`: the movement stylesheet and its script.
- `src/paint.py`: the paintings. Each scene is composed from shapes and noise, then painted with brush strokes.
- `src/people.py`: the photo placeholders, painted over a soft-focus piece of each site's own painting.
- `src/art.py`: the icons and small sketches.
- `src/trust.py`: the About, Contact, action, thank-you, not-found and policy pages, and the calendar files.
- `src/launch.py`: the launch checklist for each site. `src/prelaunch.py`: the check you run on your own copy.
- `src/audit.py`: the SEO and AI search audit that runs on every sample.
