# 001 · Coach Homepage

**Group:** Home Bases  
**For:** Coach  
**Style:** Warm Editorial (Newsreader + DM Sans)  
**Sample brand:** Wren & Field (fictional)

A personal-brand home page that names its audience in the first line, then offers 3 clear ways in: free, private and team.

## Why This Page Works

- The first line names the reader's exact situation, so the right person knows within 3 seconds that this page is for them.
- 3 doors match 3 levels of readiness: curious, ready and buying for a team. Nobody is forced to book a call to get started.
- The method has a name and a timeline. In a crowded coaching market that is easier to believe than a bigger promise.
- Every claim that matters is text, not an image: the numbers, the testimonials, the credentials. Google and AI tools can read and quote all of it.

## The Psychology At Work

| Principle | Where you can see it |
|---|---|
| **Attention Above The Fold** (Nielsen Norman Group, Scrolling and Attention (2018 eyetracking)) | The promise, the named audience, the main button and a proof number all sit in the first screen. |
| **Hick's Law** (William Hick and Ray Hyman, Laws of UX) | 3 doors, not 9. Each door states its commitment (5 minutes, 12 weeks, 6 weeks) so the choice is quick. |
| **The Power Of Free** (Dan Ariely, Predictably Irrational (2008)) | The free 5-minute check is the easy first step for visitors who are not ready to talk yet. |
| **The Unique Mechanism** (Eugene Schwartz, Breakthrough Advertising (1966)) | The Hand-Over Method gives the coaching a name and 3 dated stages. |
| **Liking** (Robert Cialdini, Influence (1984, revised 2021)) | The About strip opens with a failure, not a credential. People trust the coach who has been where they are. |
| **Reactance** (Jack Brehm, A Theory of Psychological Reactance (1966)) | The closing note says a clear no is completely fine, which lowers the guard before the call. |
| **Sitelinks Readiness** (Google Search Central, Sitelinks documentation) | The home page links to 4 distinct pages with plain names. That structure is what earns the extra links under a Google listing. |

## Page Structure

1. Hero: who it is for, the promise, 1 main action and 1 proof number, all in the first screen
2. Proof strip: where clients come from, as plain text
3. Routing cards: 3 ways in, each with its commitment stated up front
4. About strip: a real story that opens with a failure, then credentials
5. Named method: 3 stages with a timeframe, so the promise feels believable
6. Testimonials as text, each led by a specific result
7. Content list: bylined answers that give Google and AI tools something to cite
8. Email capture: a small, free, weekly yes
9. Closing call to action that protects the right to say no

## Search Card

- **Title:** Wren & Field | Leadership Coaching For First-Time Managers
- **Description:** Imogen Wren coaches new managers through their first 18 months. Private coaching, team cohorts and a free 5-minute manager check. Melbourne and online.
- **Structured data:** WebSite, Organization, Person
- WebSite, Organization and Person are linked to each other, so Google can connect the brand name to the founder's name.

## Borrow This

- Open with the reader's situation in their words, then your promise. Save your name for the second screen.
- Offer a free door, a main door and a team door. Put the time commitment on each.
- Lead each testimonial with the result as a short label, then the quote.
- Close by making it safe to say no.

## Study The Real Thing

- **April Dunford**: A 1-line positioning headline and a single action repeated down the page.
- **James Clear**: Plain noun navigation, and credibility carried by specific numbers rather than adjectives.

SEARCH-READY RULES
- 1 clear H1. A title that includes my brand name. A description that says who the page is for.
- Every word in the HTML itself. Nothing that needs JavaScript to appear.
- JSON-LD structured data (WebSite, Organization, Person) that matches the visible text.
- Canonical tag, Open Graph tags and a share image, all on 1 version of my address.
- A footer linking About, Contact, Privacy, Terms and Refunds.
- No llms.txt, no FAQ rich-result markup, no review stars on my own business, no hidden text.
- No images above the fold heavier than they need to be. Aim for the main content to load in under 2.5 seconds.

HONESTY RULES
- Use only the proof I gave you. Do not invent testimonials, client names, numbers or press.
- Where I have not given you something, leave a clearly marked placeholder.
- No fake countdown timers and no invented scarcity. If I make an income or results claim, add a line that results are not typical.

OUTPUT
1 self-contained HTML file with inline CSS. Australian spelling. No em dashes.
