How To Write A Coaching Bio That Gets You Booked

Most coaching bios list qualifications and end with a love of dogs. After writing 300 of them, here is the 4-sentence structure I use, and why it works.

By Hattie Lindqvist, copywriter for coachesPublished 6 October 20267 minute read · Updated 9 November 2026

The Short Answer

  • Sentence 1: who you help and the result they get.
  • Sentence 2: why you, in 1 specific fact.
  • Sentence 3: how you work.
  • Sentence 4: the next step. Keep the whole bio under 80 words.

I have written or rewritten 300 coaching bios since 2019. The ones that get people booked are almost never the ones the coach likes best. They are the ones a tired stranger can understand in 10 seconds.

Why Most Bios Fail

A bio is not about you. It is a reader asking 1 question: is this person for someone like me? Most bios answer a different question, which is what the coach has studied.

  • They open with a credential, not a person the reader recognises.
  • They describe a philosophy instead of a result.
  • They end without telling the reader what to do.

The 4-Sentence Structure

Here is a before and after from a client, shared with her permission. Before: 'Jo is an ICF-accredited coach passionate about helping people live their best lives.' After: 'Jo coaches nurses moving into their first management role. She spent 14 years on the wards before leading a team of 60.'

The second version has no adjectives. It has a nurse, a number and a ward.

Sentence 3 says how you work: 'She coaches 1 to 1, online, over 12 weeks.' Sentence 4 gives the next step: 'Book a 20-minute call to see if it is a fit.' That is the entire bio. 46 words.

Use The Same Bio Everywhere

Once you have it, use it word for word on your website, LinkedIn, podcast guest pages and directories. When the versions conflict, people hesitate, and so do the AI tools that now summarise who you are.

Try It Now

  • Write sentence 1 with a specific person and a specific result.
  • Cut every adjective.
  • Read it aloud to someone outside your field. If they can repeat it, you are done.
