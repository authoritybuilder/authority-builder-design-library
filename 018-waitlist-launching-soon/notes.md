# 018 · Waitlist Or Launching-Soon Page

**Group:** List Builders And Launches  
**For:** Coach, Creator, Consultant  
**Style:** Quiet Luxury (Cormorant Garamond + Jost)  
**Sample brand:** Atelier Year (fictional)

A waitlist page with almost nothing on it, on purpose. What is coming, when, why joining early is worth it, and 1 field.

## Why This Page Works

- It asks for 1 field. A waitlist is a low-commitment yes, so the form should be the lowest-effort form on the whole site.
- The benefits of joining early are specific and real: 72 hours of early access, a rate lock, a survey.
- It gives a price range before the launch. Telling people roughly what it will cost builds a waitlist of real buyers.
- It promises exactly how many emails will follow, and keeps that promise.

## The Psychology At Work

| Principle | Where you can see it |
|---|---|
| **Scarcity** (Robert Cialdini, Influence (1984, revised 2021)) | 30 founding places, an opening month and 72 hours of early access. All real. |
| **Social Proof** (Robert Cialdini, Influence (1984, revised 2021)) | 412 on the waitlist is a small, believable number shown plainly. |
| **Commitment And Consistency** (Robert Cialdini, Influence (1984, revised 2021)) | An email address now makes enrolling in April the consistent next step. |
| **IKEA Effect** (Norton, Mochon and Ariely, Journal of Consumer Psychology (2012)) | The February survey lets future members help shape the program. |
| **Anchoring** (Kahneman and Tversky, Judgment under Uncertainty (1974)) | A price range sets expectations months before the doors open. |
| **Reactance** (Jack Brehm, A Theory of Psychological Reactance (1966)) | Joining commits you to nothing, and the page says so. |
| **Aesthetic-Usability Effect** (Kurosu and Kashimura, Laws of UX) | A spare, confident page signals that the thing behind it is considered. |

## Page Structure

1. Hero: what is coming and when, with a 1-field form
2. 3 honest reasons to join early
3. A short note from the founder: why this, why now
4. The few things people need to know before giving an email

- **Description:** Atelier Year is a 12-month mentorship for independent interior designers, opening in April 2027 with 30 founding places. Join the waitlist for first access and the founding rate.
- **Structured data:** WebPage, Organization
- A launching-soon page should be indexable with a real title, so the name starts to exist in search before launch day.
- Keep this address when the program opens. Swap the form for the sales page and the early mentions keep working.

```text
Build me a waitlist or launching-soon page for my business, modelled on sample 018 in the Authority Builder Design Library.

ABOUT MY BUSINESS
- Business name and my name: [yours]
- Who I help and the result they get: [1 line]
- My main offer and its price: [yours]
- The 1 action I want a visitor to take on this page: [yours]
- Real proof I can use (names, results, numbers, press): [paste it here]

STRUCTURE, IN THIS ORDER
1. Hero: what is coming and when, with a 1-field form
2. 3 honest reasons to join early
3. A short note from the founder: why this, why now
4. The few things people need to know before giving an email

DESIGN DIRECTION
Quiet Luxury. Ivory, espresso and bronze. Thin rules, small capitals, lots of air. Says premium without shouting.
Fonts: Cormorant Garamond + Jost from Google Fonts. Keep 1 accent colour and use it only for the main action.
Mobile-first. Buttons at least 48px tall. Visible focus states. Respect reduced motion.

PERSUASION RULES
- Robert Cialdini, Scarcity: 30 founding places, an opening month and 72 hours of early access. All real.
- Robert Cialdini, Social Proof: 412 on the waitlist is a small, believable number shown plainly.
- Robert Cialdini, Commitment And Consistency: An email address now makes enrolling in April the consistent next step.
- Norton, Mochon and Ariely, IKEA Effect: The February survey lets future members help shape the program.
- Kahneman and Tversky, Anchoring: A price range sets expectations months before the doors open.
- Jack Brehm, Reactance: Joining commits you to nothing, and the page says so.
- Kurosu and Kashimura, Aesthetic-Usability Effect: A spare, confident page signals that the thing behind it is considered.

SEARCH-READY RULES
- 1 clear H1. A title that includes my brand name. A description that says who the page is for.
- Every word in the HTML itself. Nothing that needs JavaScript to appear.
- JSON-LD structured data (WebPage, Organization) that matches the visible text.
- Canonical tag, Open Graph tags and a share image, all on 1 version of my address.
- Real About, Contact, Privacy, Terms and Refund pages, linked from the footer of every page.
- A robots.txt that allows search and AI-answer crawlers and lists the sitemap, and a sitemap.xml of every real page.
- A square PNG icon (192px) at a stable address.
- No llms.txt, no FAQ rich-result markup, no review stars on my own business, no hidden text.
- No images above the fold heavier than they need to be. Aim for the main content to load in under 2.5 seconds.

CONVERSION RULES
- The main button, 1 line of reassurance and 1 piece of proof sit together in the first screen.
- Ask for as little as possible in every form. State the reply time at the button.
- Print the price. Put the guarantee or refund terms beside every buy button.
- On phones, keep the main action in a bar at the bottom of the screen.

HONESTY RULES
- Use only the proof I gave you. Do not invent testimonials, client names, numbers or press.
- Where I have not given you something, leave a clearly marked placeholder.
- No fake countdown timers and no invented scarcity. If I make an income or results claim, add a line that results are not typical.

OUTPUT
1 self-contained HTML file with inline CSS. Australian spelling. No em dashes.
```
