# 011 · Services And Pricing Page

**Group:** Offer Pages  
**For:** Consultant, Business Owner  
**Style:** Warm Editorial (Newsreader + DM Sans)  
**Sample brand:** Fieldnote Brand Studio (fictional)

A services page that ends the awkward how-much email. 3 fixed-price packages, what is and is not included, and a process with dates.

## Why This Page Works

- Prices are on the page. Buyers of professional services trust the provider who is not afraid to say the number.
- 3 packages map to 3 situations: a quick fix, a full foundation and ongoing help. The reader self-selects.
- Inclusions and exclusions are stated before anyone has to ask, which is where most service disputes start.
- An upgrade credit makes the small package a safe first step toward the large one.

## The Psychology At Work

| Principle | Where you can see it |
|---|---|
| **Anchoring** (Kahneman and Tversky, Judgment under Uncertainty (1974)) | A $25,000 agency figure frames the 3 prices that follow. |
| **Decoy Effect** (Dan Ariely, Predictably Irrational (2008)) | The Sprint and the Retainer make the Full Identity the natural centre. |
| **Framing** (Kahneman and Tversky, The Framing of Decisions (1981)) | Each price is paired with its duration: 1 day, 6 weeks, per month. |
| **Value Ladder** (Russell Brunson, DotCom Secrets (2015)) | The Sprint fee is credited toward the Full Identity, so the ladder is easy to climb. |
| **Reason-Why And Specifics** (John E. Kennedy; Claude Hopkins, Scientific Advertising (1923)) | Fixed prices come with a reason: you should know the cost before you decide. |
| **Hick's Law** (William Hick and Ray Hyman, Laws of UX) | 3 options, each with a 1-line who-it-is-for. |
| **Trust Signals** (Google, Search Quality Rater Guidelines) | Payment terms, exclusions and what happens if it goes wrong are all in view. |

## Page Structure

1. Hero: the promise of clarity. Fixed prices, visible
2. 3 packages side by side, the middle one recommended, each with a timeframe
3. What every project includes and excludes: removes scope anxiety
4. Process with real timings
5. Proof tied to each package
6. Pricing questions, answered directly
7. Closing call to action

```text
Warm Editorial. Paper tones, a bookish serif, italic emphasis. Feels like a considered letter.
Fonts: Newsreader + DM Sans from Google Fonts. Keep 1 accent colour and use it only for the main action.
Mobile-first. Buttons at least 48px tall. Visible focus states. Respect reduced motion.

PERSUASION RULES
- Kahneman and Tversky, Anchoring: A $25,000 agency figure frames the 3 prices that follow.
- Dan Ariely, Decoy Effect: The Sprint and the Retainer make the Full Identity the natural centre.
- Kahneman and Tversky, Framing: Each price is paired with its duration: 1 day, 6 weeks, per month.
- Russell Brunson, Value Ladder: The Sprint fee is credited toward the Full Identity, so the ladder is easy to climb.
- John E. Kennedy; Claude Hopkins, Reason-Why And Specifics: Fixed prices come with a reason: you should know the cost before you decide.

SEARCH-READY RULES
- 1 clear H1. A title that includes my brand name. A description that says who the page is for.
- Every word in the HTML itself. Nothing that needs JavaScript to appear.
- JSON-LD structured data (Service, Organization, BreadcrumbList) that matches the visible text.
- Canonical tag, Open Graph tags and a share image, all on 1 version of my address.
- A footer linking About, Contact, Privacy, Terms and Refunds.
- No llms.txt, no FAQ rich-result markup, no review stars on my own business, no hidden text.
- No images above the fold heavier than they need to be. Aim for the main content to load in under 2.5 seconds.

HONESTY RULES
- Use only the proof I gave you. Do not invent testimonials, client names, numbers or press.
- Where I have not given you something, leave a clearly marked placeholder.
- No fake countdown timers and no invented scarcity. If I make an income or results claim, add a line that results are not typical.

OUTPUT
1 self-contained HTML file with inline CSS. Australian spelling. No em dashes.
```
