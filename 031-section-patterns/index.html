Pattern 13 · Story or letter block  ·  used in 005, 007, 010, 018, 026

The story

The Interview Where I Stopped Apologising

In 2017 I sat in my fourth interview in a month. I had been a senior recruiter for 9 years, then a full-time parent for 6. The panel asked the question I had been dreading: so, what have you been doing?

I had a rehearsed apology ready. Instead, for reasons I still cannot explain, I told the truth. I said I had run a household budget through a redundancy, negotiated with a school board and managed a volunteer team of 40.

The room changed. They stopped interviewing a gap and started interviewing a person.

I got that job. More usefully, I got the idea that has shaped every day since: a career break is not a hole in your story. It is a chapter you have not learned to tell yet.

Two years later I left to coach full time. The method I teach now, the Chapter Method, is that interview answer turned into a process anyone can follow.

Pattern 26 · Long-form article body  ·  used in 021, 023

When Sam, the clinic's owner, first called, he wanted help with his ads. He was paying for 40 new patient enquiries a month and could not understand why the diary still had holes.

The Problem Was Not The Front Door

We spent the first week doing something unglamorous: counting. Of every 10 new patients, only 4 came back for a second appointment. The clinic was filling a bucket with a hole in it.

He did not have a marketing problem. He had a rebooking problem dressed up as a marketing problem.

What We Changed

  • Week 1: the treatment plan card. Every new patient left session 1 with a written plan: how many sessions, how far apart, and what better would look like.
  • Week 3: rebook at the table. Practitioners booked the next session before the patient stood up, not at the front desk.
  • Week 5: the day-2 message. A 2-line text from the practitioner, asking how the exercises were going.

What Happened

Rebooking went from 38% to 81% inside 6 weeks. By day 60 the diary was full enough that we cut the ad budget by two thirds. Sam hired a fourth practitioner in month 4.

What To Copy From This

  • Count before you spend. Find out where patients or clients actually drop off.
  • Give people a written plan at the first session.
  • Make the next booking part of the appointment, not an afterthought.
  • Follow up once, personally, within 2 days.
